Project 02 · Social Branding & Content

Date Drop

Building the social media branding and a short-form video presence for a University of Washington dating app — dreamy, intentional, and made for students who actually want to connect.

Role
Social Branding & Content Strategy
Timeline
2 weeks
Focus
Branding · Instagram Video · Positioning
01

The brief

Date Drop is a dating app that started out at Stanford. This project was specific to the University of Washington launch. Over the course of a month, I developed Date Drop's social media branding and content strategy around a central idea: gentle, caring intentionality from a woman's perspective. From the Instagram videos I produced to the messaging itself, every creative decision was designed to romanticize the mundane and make everyday college life feel more meaningful and fun.

My strategy centered on relatability and authenticity. Instead of high-pressure swiping or generic “hey wyd” messages, Date Drop emphasizes real compatibility through shared interests and personality traits. I also made accessibility a core quality: since most college students can't afford fancy dinners or drinks regularly, the content draws our attention to low-cost, high-connection experiences. I was inspired by the quote many girls loved from Set It Up: “Best meal I ever had was a $5 pizza thrown up on a fire escape.” This frames connection around the experience, not popularity or cost.

The goal was a presence that feels instantly different from the swipe-fast competition, and that speaks directly to students looking for something real.

02

The Research

I started by reading the market. Tinder and Bumble lean on volume and speed --> endless swiping and low-commitment matching. That gave Date Drop a clear lane to own: a slower, more intentional way to meet someone you're genuinely compatible with.

Then, I interviewed 15 college students to understand what they liked and disliked in video content, what actually caught their attention mid-scroll, and what would make them want to try a dating app based on the videos alone.

Every creative content decision tied back to that positioning.

Compatibility over volume Connection over hookups Intention over speed Student-first
03

The brand vibe

I chose three words to center this project around to guide every post, caption, and cut.

01

Dreamy

Soft, graphic-led visuals from the website give the brand a romantic, almost cinematic feel— it looks like the kind of connection users are hoping to find.

02

Intentional

The algorithm matches you with someone you're actually compatible with, so every match feels considered rather than random.

03

Mature

It isn't built for hookups. Users come looking for a real connection, and the tone of voice reflects that throughout.

04

What sets it apart

Date Drop stands out from other dating apps because it intentionally adds friction. Instead of letting people swipe endlessly, the app asks users to answer thoughtful questions and put real effort into their profiles. That extra work helps filter out people who are only there casually or looking for something quick.

For the people who stay, that effort is part of the value. It leads to more intentional matches, stronger first conversations, and a better chance of meeting someone they actually connect with, instead of just collecting more matches that go nowhere.

  • Profile effort filters out casual users before they ever match.
  • The questions signal intent, so matches start with something in common.
  • It deters the quick-fix crowd— the opposite of endless swiping.
  • This is the core differentiator the social content leads with.

The outcome

A brand that signals intention before the first swipe.

The result is a cohesive social presence that looks and feels distinct from the swipe-fast competition. It communicates Date Drop's slower, more intentional promise the moment a student finishes setting up their profile.

Paired with a content strategy the team can keep building on, the branding gives Date Drop a clear, ownable voice in a crowded category.
